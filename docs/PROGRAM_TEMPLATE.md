# PTS 6-Week Program Template — Musculoskeletal Chronic Pain (CTA-first)

Status: draft program template for therapist-led delivery.

> **Scope & safety:** This program provides structured support, skills practice, and coaching prompts. It is **not medical advice**, does not diagnose, and does not prescribe treatment. **No outcome guarantees**—only process commitments (what we practice and track). Not for emergencies.

---

## 1) Program overview

### What “CTA-first” means in this program
**CTA = Call To Action.** Each session ends with a small, clear, realistic plan the client chooses (the *next right step*), plus a simple tracking method.

CTA-first priorities:
- *Clarity:* “What exactly will I do between now and next session?”
- *Smallness:* Minimum effective dose; easier to repeat than to perfect.
- *Safety:* Conservative pacing; stop rules; red-flag routing.
- *Learning mindset:* Experiments, not tests of willpower.

### Intended outcomes (process-only)
Over 6 weeks, the client practices:
- Building a consistent routine of brief, safe self-management actions.
- CBT micro-skills to relate differently to pain-related thoughts, emotions, and avoidance.
- Physio-adjacent habits that support pacing, gentle movement tolerance, and confidence.
- A flare-up plan and a maintenance plan.

### Format (suggested)
- **Weekly therapist session:** 45–60 minutes (video/phone/in-person).
- **Between-session support:** daily micro-practice (2–10 minutes) + simple checklist.
- **Weekly check-in:** 5–10 minutes self-reflection (can be therapist-reviewed).

### Therapist workflow artifacts (repeatable)
Each week produces:
1) **Weekly CTA plan** (1–3 micro-practices + guardrails)
2) **Tracking method** (checkboxes / habit tracker)
3) **Weekly check-in** (reflection + safety flags)
4) **Therapist note** (barriers, hypotheses, next adjustment)

---

## 2) Who it’s for / not for

### Best fit (generally)
- Adults living with **persistent musculoskeletal pain** (e.g., back/neck/shoulder/hip/knee) who want structured weekly guidance.
- People who feel “stuck” in cycles of overdoing/underdoing, fear of movement, or inconsistent self-care.
- People willing to run gentle, conservative experiments and track what happens.

### Not a fit / needs different level of care
- **Emergency symptoms or red flags** (see Safety section) → urgent medical evaluation.
- Unstable severe mental health or substance use concerns needing specialized care.
- Situations requiring **medical diagnosis, medication management, or hands-on physical assessment** that cannot be safely substituted with coaching.

---

## 3) Safety & scope (read first, repeat often)

### Non-emergency disclaimer
If symptoms suggest urgent or emergency care, do **not** use this program as a substitute. Seek local urgent care/emergency services.

### “Stop rules” during any practice
Stop the activity and shift to safety steps if any of the below occurs:
- Sudden severe pain, pain that escalates quickly, or pain that feels “wrong” (sharp/tearing/electric) rather than tolerable discomfort.
- New or worsening numbness, tingling, weakness, loss of coordination, or spreading symptoms.
- Dizziness, faintness, shortness of breath, chest pain, or feeling unsafe.
- Any symptom that triggers fear of serious harm.

### Red flags to watch (non-exhaustive)
Escalate to medical evaluation (urgent if severe) if any occur:
- New bowel/bladder dysfunction, saddle anesthesia, rapidly progressive weakness.
- Fever, chills, unexplained weight loss, history of cancer with new unexplained pain.
- Significant recent trauma (fall, accident) with severe pain.
- Unrelenting night pain, pain with systemic illness, or suspected infection.
- Chest pain, severe shortness of breath, signs of stroke, or other medical emergencies.

### Escalation phrasing (therapist script)
Use language like:
- “Given what you described, I’m not comfortable treating this as a self-management issue. I recommend you seek **in-person medical assessment** as soon as possible.”
- “This program can support coping and routine, but **it can’t rule out medical causes**. Let’s prioritize safety and get you checked.”

## 4) Measurement (tracking without promises)

Measure to learn and adapt—not to prove improvement.

### Core metrics (2–3)
Collect at baseline (Week 1) and weekly (Weeks 2–6):
1) **Self-efficacy (0–10):** “How confident am I that I can do my plan this week?”
2) **Pain interference (0–10):** “How much did pain interfere with what matters to me this week?”
3) **Adherence (0–100%):** “Of the days I planned a micro-practice, how many did I complete?”

Optional (if useful): sleep quality (0–10), flare-up recovery confidence (0–10), avoidance behaviors (brief checklist).

### Weekly check-in (client-facing)
- Confidence (0–10)
- Interference (0–10)
- What worked? What got in the way?
- Any new symptoms or safety concerns?

## 5) Weekly structure (Weeks 1–6)

Each week includes:
- **Objectives** (process-only)
- **Session themes (CTA-first)**
- **CBT micro-skill** (teach + practice in-session)
- **Physio-adjacent coaching checklist** (safe, non-prescriptive)
- **Between-session practices** (1–3 micro-actions)
- **Reflection prompts** (weekly check-in)

> **Standing guardrails (copy/paste every week):**
> - Stay conservative: aim for “doable” not “heroic.”
> - Stop if symptoms worsen or feel unsafe; use stop rules.
> - If red flags are present, prioritize medical evaluation.

---

### Week 1 — Orientation, safety boundaries, and a tiny baseline

**Objectives (process):**
- Establish scope/safety, expectations, and tracking.
- Build a baseline routine that feels safe and repeatable.
- Identify top 1–2 activities pain interferes with (function targets).

**Session themes (CTA-first):**
- Primary CTA: **Choose 1 daily micro-practice + 1 “movement experiment”** for 7 days.
- Secondary CTA: **Write a 2-sentence flare response plan** (“If I flare, I will…”) 

**CBT micro-skill:** *Pain cycle mapping (thought–feeling–behavior loop)*
- Map one recent flare/avoidance moment.
- Identify one “leverage point” behavior to change (smaller/safer).

**Physio-adjacent coaching checklist (safe):**
- Screen for red flags + stop rules.
- Clarify “hurt vs harm” carefully (without medical claims): “some discomfort can occur; we stay within safe, conservative limits and adjust.”
- Baseline movement inventory: what’s currently tolerated (walking, stairs, sitting time, chores).
- Pacing intro: avoid boom–bust; use **time-based** or **reps-based** caps.

**Between-session practices (pick 1–3):**
- Daily micro-practice (2–5 min): *Breathing + downshift* (e.g., slow exhale, 6 breaths).
- Daily movement experiment (5–10 min): *Gentle walk or gentle mobility within comfort* (client-chosen).
- 1-minute note: “What did I do today that supported me?”

**Reflection prompts:**
- What felt doable? What felt too much?
- What did you notice about the pain-avoidance loop?
- Any safety concerns or new symptoms?

---

### Week 2 — Pacing and consistency (protect the routine)

**Objectives (process):**
- Strengthen routine consistency and reduce boom–bust patterns.
- Create a personalized pacing rule for 1–2 daily activities.

**Session themes (CTA-first):**
- Primary CTA: **Set a pacing cap** for one activity that commonly triggers a flare.
- Secondary CTA: **Schedule micro-practice at a stable cue** (after tea/after brushing).

**CBT micro-skill:** *Behavioral experiment framing*
- “We’re collecting data, not chasing a perfect day.”
- Predict → do → observe → adjust.

**Physio-adjacent coaching checklist (safe):**
- Review last week’s tolerability; adjust dose down if needed.
- Define pacing cap options:
  - time cap (e.g., 10 minutes then break)
  - break schedule (e.g., 2 minutes rest per 10 minutes activity)
  - split tasks (two short blocks vs one long)
- Introduce “rate of perceived exertion” style check-in (easy/moderate/hard) to avoid overdoing.

**Between-session practices:**
- Pacing plan for one task (written): “I will stop at X, not at pain.”
- Micro-practice (2–5 min): *Urge surfing for ‘push through’ or ‘avoid’ urges*.
- Movement experiment: maintain or slightly reduce dose for reliability.

**Reflection prompts:**
- Where did pacing help most?
- What cue worked best for consistency?
- What got in the way of stopping on time?

---

### Week 3 — Cognitive flexibility (reduce threat amplification)

**Objectives (process):**
- Identify common unhelpful pain thoughts.
- Practice one cognitive flexibility tool in real moments.

**Session themes (CTA-first):**
- Primary CTA: **Use one “thought label” tool once per day** (30–60 seconds).
- Secondary CTA: **Create one coping statement** that feels credible (not forced optimism).

**CBT micro-skill:** *“Catch it, Check it, Choose” (mini thought record)*
- Catch: What’s the thought?
- Check: What evidence supports/doesn’t support it?
- Choose: What’s a more balanced next-step thought?

**Physio-adjacent coaching checklist (safe):**
- Link cognition to behavior: threat thoughts → guarding/avoidance → deconditioning.
- Rehearse “safe range” language: “Within my comfort range, small movement is allowed.”
- Reinforce conservative movement choice; no pushing through sharp pain.

**Between-session practices:**
- 1×/day “Catch it, Check it, Choose” on a real pain moment.
- Movement experiment: keep dose stable; add **one confidence rep** (e.g., 30 seconds more) only if last week was consistently tolerable.

**Reflection prompts:**
- What thought showed up most often?
- Which reframe was believable and helpful?
- Did the reframe change what you did next?

---

### Week 4 — Graded exposure to a valued activity (conservative)

**Objectives (process):**
- Choose one avoided activity tied to values (work, social, household, movement).
- Create a stepwise exposure plan with safety guardrails.

**Session themes (CTA-first):**
- Primary CTA: **Do Step 1 of the exposure ladder 3× this week.**
- Secondary CTA: **Plan a “recovery routine”** after exposure (downshift + rest).

**CBT micro-skill:** *Exposure ladder + prediction testing*
- List 5–7 steps from easiest to hardest.
- Before/after ratings: fear (0–10), confidence (0–10), interference (0–10).

**Physio-adjacent coaching checklist (safe):**
- Choose exposure target that is meaningful and low-risk.
- Define “success” as *showing up*, not symptom elimination.
- Dose planning: start below what the client thinks they can do.
- Emphasize form: steady breathing, relaxed pace, breaks.

**Between-session practices:**
- Exposure Step 1 (3×) + quick notes: What happened? What did you learn?
- Micro-practice (2–5 min): *Self-compassionate coaching voice* (“I’m practicing safely”).

**Reflection prompts:**
- What did you predict would happen? What actually happened?
- What made the exposure feel safer?
- What is the next step that’s still conservative?

---

### Week 5 — Flare-up plan + problem solving barriers

**Objectives (process):**
- Build a personalized, non-emergency flare-up playbook.
- Identify and solve one recurring barrier to adherence.

**Session themes (CTA-first):**
- Primary CTA: **Write and use your flare-up plan once** (even as a rehearsal).
- Secondary CTA: **Implement one barrier fix** (environment, reminders, social support).

**CBT micro-skill:** *Structured problem solving (IDEA)*
- Identify the barrier.
- Develop options (at least 3).
- Evaluate best small option.
- Act and review.

**Physio-adjacent coaching checklist (safe):**
- Flare-up playbook elements (non-prescriptive):
  - Reduce load temporarily (not to zero unless necessary)
  - Short, gentle movement within comfort (if tolerable)
  - Downshift (breathing/relaxation)
  - Heat/cold only if already known safe for the client; avoid medical claims
  - Sleep and routine protection
  - Red-flag check + stop rules
- Clarify “flare ≠ failure” framing.

**Between-session practices:**
- Flare-up plan card (client-written):
  1) “First 10 minutes I will…”
  2) “Today I will keep doing…” (minimum routine)
  3) “If X happens, I will seek care / contact clinician.”
- Barrier fix experiment (one change) + track impact.

**Reflection prompts:**
- What early signs tell you a flare may be coming?
- Which part of the plan helped you feel more in control?
- What barrier fix was most effective?

---

### Week 6 — Consolidation, maintenance, and next steps

**Objectives (process):**
- Consolidate what works into a sustainable plan.
- Create a maintenance schedule and “when-to-escalate” criteria.
- Prepare for independence or ongoing support cadence.

**Session themes (CTA-first):**
- Primary CTA: **Choose a 4-week maintenance plan** (2–3 practices).
- Secondary CTA: **Define your relapse/flare response** + who to contact.

**CBT micro-skill:** *Relapse prevention & values-based planning*
- Identify high-risk situations (stress, travel, workload).
- Pre-commit coping responses.
- Reconnect to values: “Why do I practice?”

**Physio-adjacent coaching checklist (safe):**
- Review tolerability trend and confidence trend (not just pain intensity).
- Set conservative progression rules (only one change at a time; return to baseline if symptoms worsen).
- Encourage appropriate medical/physio follow-up if needed for assessment.

**Between-session practices:**
- Maintenance plan written + calendar cues.
- “If–Then” plans:
  - “If I miss 2 days, then I restart with the smallest version.”
  - “If I flare, then I use the flare-up plan and reassess.”

**Reflection prompts:**
- What are your top 3 learnings?
- What are your early warning signs, and what will you do first?
- What support (if any) would you like going forward?

## 7) Implementation notes (product/workflow)

- Keep language **non-prescriptive**: “consider,” “experiment,” “within comfort,” “choose what’s safe for you.”
- Avoid diagnosis labels; anchor on **function + interference**.
- Reuse the same guardrails each week for consistency.
- Prefer 1–3 practices max; more options can reduce adherence.
